Project title: Effects of β-casein-derived bioactive peptides on gut microbiota in healthy Saudi children

Project Co PIs: Areej Almuraee, and Professor.  Glenn Gibson
 Hafiz

Schools: Food Biosciences

Casein, the dominant protein in milk, forms complex micelles that are made from a mixture of three caseins. In particular, bovine beta-casein is known to be a source of biologically active peptides which may exert pharmacological activities. BCM7 is one of the peptides that ecological studies have suggested that this peptide may be involved in the aetiology of a range of chronic diseases, including Type 1 diabetes, ischaemic heart disease, autism and schizophrenia. It can also involve in the gastrointestinal function and immunity. The aim of this study is to investigate how beta-caseins from 2 alleles, A1 and A2 are likely to impact on the colonic microbiota using in vitro batch cultures. Bovine milk was digested in vitro and then tested the impact of A1/A2 and A2/A2 digested milk on the gut microbiota derived from 3 young healthy donors using validated in vitro pH controlled batch culture system over a period of 48 h. Extracted DNA for DNA sequencing for deep coverage of the microbial population will allow us to see if there is any different between treatments and if BCM-5 and 7 has an impact on gut health.
